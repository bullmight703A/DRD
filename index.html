About

I have sat in the rooms where these decisions actually get made.

My work sits at the intersection of cybersecurity, governance, technology and organizational leadership.

Across two decades of complex technology and regulatory environments, I have worked with executives, engineers, security teams, public-sector stakeholders and business leaders to translate technical requirements, organizational realities and risk into decisions people can actually act on.

That practitioner perspective shapes how I write, speak and advise. Controls matter. Governance matters. Technology matters. But none of them create value on their own. Leadership is what turns them into outcomes.

I bring the perspective of an executive practitioner, not a commentator observing the work from the outside.

What's on my mind

Every organization I work with is being asked to move faster than its controls, governance and decision rights were designed for. AI accelerated that gap.

The work I find most valuable is not choosing between speed and safety. It is building the operating discipline that lets leaders move fast and still explain, defend and stand behind every consequential decision.

Does it still work? Could it be better? Is it creating value?
